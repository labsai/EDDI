## Against the baseline

| Concurrent conv. | Baseline turns/s · p50 · p95 | WS2 turns/s · p50 · p95 |
|---|---|---|
| 200 | 52–60 · 3.1–3.3 s · 3.5–4.4 s | 60 · 3.1 s · 3.6 s |
| 500 | 61 · 7.5 s · 9.8–10.1 s | **131 · 3.3 s · 4.2 s** |
| 1000 | not run (past the ceiling) | **269 · 3.2 s · 4.6 s** |

- **The thread ceiling is gone.** The managed pool carries no turns any more (peak 0 active, 0
  queued). Turns peaked at 1029 on `pool="turn"` with about 320 live JVM threads, and throughput
  now grows with concurrency at the LLM's pace (≈ conversations / 3.2 s). This meets WS2's acceptance
  criterion of ≥ 1000 in-flight turns on one node.
- **Overload is a clean rejection.** At 1500 conversations in-flight turns stopped at exactly 1000;
  the excess got 8161 × 503 `capacity_exceeded` (`eddi_turn_rejected_total{reason="in_flight_limit"}`
  8161), with no timeouts and no other 5xx. Turns/s is lower than at 1000 because k6 retries a
  refused turn instantly, without backoff, so the node spends work on refusals.
  `active{pool="turn"}` briefly exceeds the limit (1029): a turn's permit is released when the
  client gets its answer, and persistence follows on the same thread.
- **MongoDB operations per OK turn are unchanged** (17.5 queries, 2.0 updates, 3.2 inserts at
  1000 conversations). In the overload run they rise to 25 queries per OK turn, because a refused
  turn has already loaded the conversation. Admission sits after those loads, which is what WS3a
  (one load per turn) addresses.
- The 50-conversation p95 (5.6 s) is the first run after start (JIT warm-up), not a regression.
  The baseline's second run showed the same spread.
- Audit: peak queue 3206 of 100000 at 1000 conversations, nothing dropped.
