## feat(attachments): quota reservations and one lock order for every store write (2026-11-21)

**Repo:** EDDI (`feat/doc-quota-reservations`). This is work package WP-2a.3 of the PDF documents plan, the third package in the serial chain of attachment-store edits (after WP-2a.2, before WP-4.1b). It depends on WP-2a.2 (#1502) and, through it, on WP-0.1 (#1461) and WP-2a.1 (#1471). Nothing user-visible: no code path takes a reservation yet; the streaming upload (WP-4.1b) and the document jobs (WP-4.3) will.

### What changed and why

A write that runs too long to hold the 60 s quota lock — a streaming upload, a background job writing a file — can now **hold quota without holding a lock**.

- **`IAttachmentStore`** gains `reserve(OwnerRef, conversationId, userId, bytes, count, ttl)`, `release(id)` and `convert(id, storageRef)`, with the new record `QuotaReservation(id, ownerKey, bytes, count, expiresAt)`. A reservation is written under the bucket locks after the same check an upload makes, and from then on **counts as usage** of its conversation and user buckets — for uploads, derived media and other reservations alike — until it is converted, released or expires.
- **Usage = stored rows + live reservations** at every quota check: upload, derived media and reserve, on both stores. On PostgreSQL the two are read in one statement (one snapshot); on MongoDB the reservations are read before the files, both from the primary, so a conversion between the two reads is counted at least once, never zero times.
- **Expiry by the database's clock.** PostgreSQL compares `expires_at` with `NOW()`; MongoDB writes and compares with `$$NOW`, never a node's clock, so clock skew between nodes cannot stretch or cut a reservation. PostgreSQL's `convert` checks the expiry with `clock_timestamp()`, not `NOW()`, which is the time its transaction began and may be older than the locks it waited for. Every reserve, with quotas on or off, sweeps expired reservations of any bucket: on PostgreSQL at most 100 per call with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, so two sweeps never wait on each other, backed by an index on `expires_at`; on MongoDB a TTL index on `expiresAt` is the backstop.
- **`convert`** checks, under the reservation's bucket locks, that the reservation is still live, that the blob is a root in the same conversation and user buckets, and that it is no larger than what was reserved; then the reservation goes and the blob counts in its place. The blob must be visible first, so usage reads "both" for a moment, never "neither". On PostgreSQL the conversion also runs inside a caller's transaction, which is how the streaming store will make it atomic with its insert.
- **One lock order: conversation → user → team → source** (`AttachmentLockOrder`, plan X14/P8). The stores have always locked conversation before user, so the scopes added since come after them, and an old node and a new one cannot wait on each other in a cycle. All writers (`store`, `storeDerived`, `reserve`, `convert`) now build their lock lists through it. The lock keys are byte-for-byte the ones earlier releases use; a unit test pins them. No team lock is taken yet.
- **Schema.** PostgreSQL: table `attachment_reservations (id, owner_key, conversation_id, user_id, bytes, count, expires_at, created_at)` with indexes on `conversation_id`, `user_id` and `expires_at`, created in `ensureSchema()`. MongoDB: collection `attachment_reservations` with a TTL index on `expiresAt` and indexes on `conversationId` and `userId`, created at startup by `GridFsIndexInitializer` in their own time budget. Both are new and empty; no migration.
- **Erasure.** `deleteByConversation` deletes the conversation's reservations on both stores, before its files: deleted after them, a blob stored after the file scan could still be converted and survive the erasure. A GDPR erasure during an upload leaves no quota held (plan §3.3 matrix row "erase during an upload"). `docs/gdpr-compliance.md` says so.
- **`AttachmentQuotas.check`** gains an overload for several files at once (or none: a bytes-only reservation), and `AttachmentQuotaExceededException` the scope `reservation` for a write that outlived or outgrew its reservation.

### Design decisions

- **Only conversation owners reserve in this release.** `USER` and `TEAM` owners are refused with `IllegalArgumentException`: their library buckets (WP-5a.1, WP-5b.1) do not exist yet, and charging a library write to a conversation bucket would be wrong. `conversationId` must be the owner's key; a `null` `userId` exempts the reservation from the per-user quota, as for an upload. Lifetimes are capped at 24 hours (`QuotaReservation.MAX_TTL`). One reservation holds at most 1 TiB and 10,000 files (`MAX_BYTES`, `MAX_COUNT`), and `AttachmentQuotas.check` compares `incoming > limit - used`, so no amount a caller passes can overflow a quota sum and pass the check.
- **No erasure participant.** Every reservation has a conversation, so the conversation cascade reaches it, and an orphan stops counting when it expires and is swept by the next reserve. The library packages, whose reservations will be owner-keyed, add theirs (WP-5a.4).
- **Converting an expired or released reservation is refused** (`reservation` scope) rather than silently accepted: the blob was then never covered by quota, and its writer must delete it.
- **`reserve` declares `AttachmentStoreException`**, not only `AttachmentQuotaExceededException` as the plan's signature says: a store failure or a MongoDB lock that cannot be taken in time is not a quota refusal.
- **Platform Operator, MCP, UI: no change.** No endpoint, tool, property or designer key is added, and nothing takes a reservation yet. `operator-revision.json` is not bumped.

### Tests

New `QuotaReservationContract`, run on both databases by `GridFsAttachmentStoreIT` and `PostgresAttachmentStoreIT`: reservations count toward conversation bytes, user bytes across conversations, file counts, other reservations and derived media; the stored lifetime is exactly the one asked for, on the database's clock; expiry frees the quota, and every reserve sweeps expired rows of any bucket, also with quotas off; release is idempotent; convert hands the reservation's place to its blob, and is refused before the blob exists, for a blob in other buckets, for an expired or released reservation, and for a blob larger than reserved; deleting a conversation deletes its reservations; only a conversation's own quota can be reserved. Two concurrency tests repeat 20 times each: 20 writers, half reserving and half uploading, against room for exactly ten admit exactly ten; and reservations, uploads and derived media on the same buckets never deadlock. `AttachmentLockOrderTest`, `QuotaReservationTest` (caps, lifetime order, overflow-safe arithmetic) and a lock-key test per store cover the pure parts. The mutants run are listed in the PR.
